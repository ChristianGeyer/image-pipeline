Title:
Deep Learning-Based Bullet Hole Detection in Outdoor Shooting Ranges Using Mask R-CNN and ResNet

Authors:
Ahmed et al.

Year:
2023

Input:
RGB images of paper shooting targets captured in outdoor military shooting ranges. Images include difficult conditions such as shadows, rain, dirt, uneven lighting, and background clutter.

Method:
Two-stage deep learning pipeline:

1) Target segmentation:
   A Mask R-CNN network is trained to segment the paper target from the background. This isolates the green paper target and removes environmental clutter such as grass, sky, and target holders.

2) Bullet hole detection:
   A ResNet-50 CNN is trained on cropped target regions to classify and localize bullet holes. The network treats bullet holes as visual objects and outputs their positions.

The system does not use any geometric model of the target or scoring rings.

Dataset:
- 400 real outdoor images of shooting targets
- Images collected under varying weather and lighting conditions
- Two separate datasets:
  - One for training Mask R-CNN (target segmentation)
  - One for training ResNet-50 (bullet hole detection)

Evaluation metrics:
- Mask R-CNN: mean Average Precision (mAP) for target segmentation
- ResNet-50: classification accuracy for bullet hole detection

Accuracy:
- Target segmentation (Mask R-CNN): mAP ≈ 83%
- Bullet hole detection (ResNet-50): accuracy ≈ 86%

These values are significantly lower than indoor CNN systems but demonstrate robustness to outdoor conditions.

Speed:
Not explicitly reported. The system requires two CNN passes (segmentation + detection), making it heavier than single-stage detectors like YOLO.

Key idea:
Use deep learning to handle difficult outdoor environments by first isolating the target with instance segmentation and then detecting bullet holes inside the segmented region.

Limitations:
- Does not compute bullet hole center with geometric precision
- Does not perform ISSF-compliant scoring
- No use of target ring dimensions
- Accuracy is limited compared to indoor CNN-based systems
- Requires two neural networks, increasing computational cost

Relevance to my thesis:
This paper shows that CNN-based approaches can robustly detect targets and bullet holes in uncontrolled outdoor conditions, but they do not provide the geometric accuracy needed for ISSF scoring. This supports the need for a hybrid system: CNN for robust detection combined with classical geometry for precise scoring.
